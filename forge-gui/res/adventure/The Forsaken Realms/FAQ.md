## Card sets and Research

### How does Research work? How do I unlock more card sets?

You only start with a few sets open. Research opens the rest.

- **Starting sets:** each race has 4 sets (see [Races](#races)). You start with all 4 on Easy, 3 on Normal, 2 on Hard
  and 1 on Insane, picked at random from your race's 4.
- **Collect first:** a set becomes researchable once you own 10% of its cards (minimum 5). A card counts toward the set
  it's printed in, and duplicates count too. The game tells you when you cross that line.
- **Where:** the **Research Lab** in your Capitol, so you need a Capitol first. The Lab lists each set with your
  progress as (owned/needed).
- **Cost:** 50 Shards on Normal (38 on Easy, 63 on Hard, 75 on Insane).
- **Time:** 7 in-game days. You can research several sets at the same time, each on its own timer, and it finishes by
  itself.

**What unlocking a set does:**

- Your own shops (your card shops, plus the Capitol's Booster Shop and Spellsmith) can now stock it. Your shops sell
  ONLY your unlocked sets, but they're 25% cheaper than neutral towns and 40% cheaper than AI towns (before
  reputation).
- Your towns' Inn tournaments can use it. Every other Inn also mixes your unlocked sets into its pool.
- It does NOT change what AI or neutral towns sell, what enemies drop or what chests hold. Those follow the world's
  set split (see [Card set distribution](#card-set-distribution)).
- It never limits your deck: every card you own is always playable.

## Challenge Coins

### What does the Bronze Coin do? What about the Silver and Gold coins?

Your starting kit includes 1 Gold, 1 Silver and 3 Bronze Challenge Coins.

- **Gold:** free entry to a Draft tournament at an Inn.
- **Silver:** free entry to a Sealed tournament.
- **Bronze, use 1: free Jumpstart entry** instead of the 500 gold or 50 Shard entry fee. You only ever get ONE
  Jumpstart tournament per player (New Game+ included), so only one Bronze coin can go this way.
- **Bronze, use 2: ante ransom.** Lose an ordinary duel while playing for ante, and the "Card Lost" screen offers
  **Bronze Coin**. You get back every card you anted in that match (every game of a best-of-3) and you keep your
  gold. The life loss still applies.

**Getting a Bronze coin back, or a new one:**

- The enemy keeps your coin. Beat any enemy of that kind again (any Fox, if a Fox took it) and the coin comes back as
  loot.
- Or use the **Coin Challenge** in your Capitol's Arena once it's Level 2: 50 gold on Easy/Normal or 100 on
  Hard/Insane, plus 5/10/15 Shards on Normal/Hard/Insane. No ante, losing costs nothing more, one try per opponent
  each week.
- Or buy one: your own **Armory** can stock it as a Mythic item (list price 1,000 gold), from week 3 in your Capitol
  and week 4 in your other towns.

**Rules:**

- One coin per kind of enemy: while a Fox holds one, the ransom isn't offered against Foxes.
- Never offered against bosses, in the Arena or in tournaments.
- It's offered on the ante screen, so it needs Play for Ante on (the default).
- Winning 2+ rounds of a Draft pays a Gold coin; 2+ wins in Sealed pays a Silver coin.
- New Game+ tops you back up to 1 Gold / 1 Silver / 3 Bronze, and coins enemies were holding are forgotten.

## Losing and winning

### How do I lose the game?

Losing duels does NOT end your run. Only three things do:

1. **Your Capitol falls.** A war mage that gets past your Capitol's guards (and isn't turned away by your Capitol's
   own town reputation) forces you into a best-of-3 duel, wherever you are. Lose it and the run is over.
2. **One AI color holds 3 of the 5 Ring Cities** around the campfire (Benalia, Tolaria, Urborg, Shiv, Llanowar). You
   get a warning when one color reaches 2.
3. **No free town is left:** an AI capture leaves no wasteland town anywhere (working, ruined, or an unclaimed Ring
   City) while you hold no town and no Capitol.

A defeat screen then returns you to the main menu.

**What a lost duel costs:** 2% / 5% / 10% / 15% of the gold you carry, but at least 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 gold
(Easy / Normal / Hard / Insane; everything you carry if you have less; gold in the bank is safe), 10% / 20% / 30% / 30% of your max life, and your ante (see the Bronze coin). If your life hits 0
you're carried home: to your Capitol, or to Orazca if you don't have one yet. Losing inside a rotating dungeon usually
makes that dungeon vanish.

**How to win:** hold all five Ring Cities AND bring down all five colors by beating the Lord in each color's castle.
You can keep playing afterward.

## Card set distribution

### Why can't I find cards from set XYZ?

Every new world deals the card sets out at random:

- 12 sets go to a **neutral** pool, and the rest are split evenly among the five **AI colors** (about 35 each).
- **Your race's 4 sets** never go to an AI color. They're added to the neutral pool.
- It's re-dealt for every new world: XYZ can be Red's in one game, Blue's in the next.

**Where cards come from:**

- **An AI color's towns and capital:** that color's sets.
- **Neutral towns:** the neutral pool.
- **Your towns and Capitol:** only the sets YOU have unlocked (see [Research](#card-sets-and-research)).
- **Ring Cities:** every set, at double price.
- **Enemy drops:** the sets of the enemy's color (its main color if it has several). Colorless enemies use the neutral
  pool. Bosses and special fighters can drop anything.
- **Dungeon chests:** the sets of whoever owns the land the dungeon is on (the neutral pool on wasteland and your own
  land). Some have their own themed chests.
- **Archaeologist** (Capitol): cards you don't own, from any set.
- **Inns:** yours use your race's sets plus your unlocked sets. Other Inns use your unlocked sets plus the neutral
  pool.

**Also good to know:**

- A card belongs to every set it was printed in. Any source holding one of those sets can give it to you (as that
  set's printing).
- At **War** with a color you're barred from its towns (its capital charges 500 gold to enter).
- A shop's type (say, Goblins) plus its owner's sets can make a short shelf.
- Never dealt: sets with no booster packs, Un-sets and other novelty/promo sets, and Alpha, Beta and Unlimited (their
  cards still turn up as later printings). A short ban list (Power 9, Sol Ring, Signets, Commander-only cards...) is
  kept out of rewards and shops.
- Advanced: the game folder's content filter tables let you switch sets, items or enemies off yourself (all on by
  default).

## Races

### What does my race change?

Your race decides your look, your 4 card sets and 2 of your 5 starting shop blueprints. It doesn't change any stats.

- **Sets:** you start with some of your race's 4 sets unlocked (4 on Easy, 3 Normal, 2 Hard, 1 Insane, picked at
  random). Standard, Constructed and Pile starting decks are built from your race's sets. No AI color ever holds them
  (they sit in the neutral pool), and your own towns' Inn tournaments use them.
- **Blueprints:** your race's 2 tribal shops, plus your color's 3 Common shops.
- The **?** button on the race screen lists the sets before you commit.

## Shops and Blueprints

### How do shops work in my towns and Capitol?

- **Slots:** each town you own has 9 building slots (8 open slots and an Armory). Your Capitol has 24: 16 open, plus
  5 colored Land Shops, a Utility Land Shop, a Booster Shop and an Armory.
- **Building:** walk into a rubble slot and pick Card Shop > tier > category (By Color, By Card Type, Tribal,
  Special) > type. An open slot can also become a Mine, a Trader, an Outlook and so on.
- **Tier cost** (Normal; x0.75 on Easy, x1.25 Hard, x1.5 Insane):
  - Common: 100 gold + 5 wood
  - Uncommon: 150 gold + 10 wood
  - Rare: 200 gold + 50 Shards, **Capitol only**
  - Mythic: 300 gold + 100 Shards, **Capitol only**
- **Rules:** you can only build types you have the blueprint for, and each type only once per town. The menu still
  lists every type: buildable ones first, then "(built)", then grayed "(locked)", each showing how many cards it could
  stock for you right now.
- **Stock:** your shops sell only cards from your unlocked sets. Every shop restocks weekly, and the Refresh button
  re-rolls it now for a few Shards (+1 for each refresh that week).
- **Re-assign Shop Type** (a button inside your own card shops): switch to another type you know. You're credited half
  the old shop's gold cost.
- **Destroy Building** clears a slot, with no refund.
- **Town reputation caps building:** each point of a town's own reputation allows 3 buildings there. You get +1 for
  restoring the town, +2 when it becomes your Capitol, +1 for each attacking mage you beat there, and more from its Job
  Board quests.
- **Capitol Land Shops:** a colored Land Shop can be repaired (50 gold) once you've visited that color's AI capital.

### How do Blueprints work?

A blueprint teaches you a card shop type (there are about 250) so you can build it in your own towns.

- **You start with 5:** your color's 3 Common shops and your race's 2 tribal shops. Your color's 3 Uncommon shops and
  its Rare shop are the ladder to climb. (A Chaos or Custom start has no color, so it only gets the race shops.)
- **Buy the shop you're standing in:** almost any card shop whose type you don't know shows a **Buy Blueprint**
  button - in AI towns and capitals, neutral towns, even your own towns.
- **Price, in Shards:** 20 Common, 40 Uncommon, 100 Rare, 200 Mythic. The tier is the type's tier in your own build
  menu, so a blueprint costs the same in every town that sells it.
- **Reputation gate (the five colors' towns only):** you must be at least Neutral with that color to buy any
  blueprint, Happy for Uncommon, and Partner for Rare and Mythic. Happy takes 15% off the price, Partner 30%. Neutral
  towns have no gate.
- **The Mythic tier** (Capitol only) holds the five-color, three-color, Phyrexian, Planeswalker and Legend shops.
- **Drops:** Mystery pickups (the diamond) and Chests each have a 25% chance to give you a random blueprint you don't
  have yet. An Archaeologist expedition has a 15% chance.
- **Not learnable:** the Cartographer's Guild basic-land shops, Armories and Ring City shops.
- Blueprints are for building only. You can shop in any store you can enter, blueprint or not.

## Reputation

### How does Reputation work?

You have a separate standing with each of the five colors (see the World Standings screen). There are five statuses:

- **Partner (80+):** card shops in that color's towns 30% off. Its war mages are 75% less likely to pick your towns.
  Entering its towns heals you to full plus 2 extra life until your next duel. Its Spellsmiths serve you, and all its
  blueprints are for sale, Rare and Mythic included.
- **Happy (30 to 79):** 15% off. Mages 50% less likely. Its Spellsmiths serve you. Common and Uncommon blueprints for
  sale.
- **Neutral (-29 to 29):** normal prices. Common blueprints only.
- **Unhappy (-30 to -79):** 25% dearer. Mages 15% more likely. No blueprints, and entering its towns no longer heals
  you.
- **War (-80 or worse):** its towns bar you (you can attack one instead, once a week per town). Its capital charges a
  500 gold toll and prices there are 40% dearer. Mages 50% more likely, and it may also target your Capitol. No
  healing in its towns.

Standing also changes:

- **Travel speed** on that color's land: +10% Partner, +5% Happy, -5% Unhappy, -10% War.
- **Its creatures:** at Partner you meet them a third as often, at War three times as often. On its land they also
  lean weaker when it likes you and tougher when it doesn't. Only at Unhappy and War do legends appear on its land -
  rarely, the ones you haven't met first, never two of the same at once. A legend holds its ground for 3 days until
  you come close (your quest log shows which way it is and how long it has left) and starts its duels with a
  Gemstone Mine in play. At War its arena champions roam too.

None of this touches your own towns. Each town also keeps its own **town reputation**, separate from the colors: it
shifts prices in that town by up to 10%, and in your own towns it caps how many buildings you can have and helps fend
off war mages.

### What is the "Net-0" rule? Why does beating one color change the others?

Reputation is never created, only moved around the color wheel, so your five standings add up to zero.

| Color | Allies | Enemies |
|---|---|---|
| White | Green, Blue | Black, Red |
| Blue | White, Black | Red, Green |
| Black | Blue, Red | Green, White |
| Red | Black, Green | White, Blue |
| Green | Red, White | Blue, Black |

**Every duel you win** against a creature of one color:

- that color **-2**
- each of its 2 allies **-1**
- each of its 2 enemies **+2**

That's -2 -1 -1 +2 +2 = 0. A boss counts x3 and an attacking war mage x2. A multicolor creature applies half the
pattern for each of its colors. Colorless creatures, losses, Arena and tournament games change nothing.

**Example, mono-Red starting deck:** your deck seeds Red +10, its allies Black and Green +5 each, its enemies White
and Blue -10 each (total 0). You beat a mono-Black creature: Black -2, Blue -1, Red -1, Green +2, White +2. Now:
White -8, Blue -11, Black +3, Red +9, Green +7. Total: still 0.

**What that means for you:**

- Fighting your own color's creatures annoys your own color.
- To raise Red, beat White and Blue creatures: +2 Red per win, but White and Blue sink with every one.
- You can't be everyone's Partner. If one color is at +80, the other four share about -80.
- Attacking a color's town costs 4 with it (its allies -2, its enemies +4). Capturing the town costs 8 more.
- The only exceptions: a few quests give a small flat bonus (+1 or +2), and bringing down a color's castle costs you a
  flat 50 with that color.

## Dungeons and the map

### Why did a dungeon disappear from my map?

Dungeons and caves rotate. Each one lasts 20-40 in-game days, then vanishes, and a new one opens somewhere else. One
also leaves early when:

- you clear it out,
- you lose a duel inside it (a side-quest target gives you 3 tries and 30 extra days; story locations never vanish),
- you take all its loot while its enemies still stand: then it keeps only a quarter of its remaining days.

Boss lairs are different: they only leave once cleared, and come back 10-30 days later, restocked. Return visits pay
half, and a +Life reward or a boss's own signature item only pays once.

### Why is most of the map black?

That's Fog of War (on by default; you can turn it off in Settings). Black = never explored, dimmed = explored but out
of sight, bright = what you can see right now. Creatures only show in bright areas. Your own land and towns stay lit,
and an Outlook widens a town's view (x2, or x3 in your Capitol). A Torch in your ability slot doubles your sight (a
Grand Torch x4), and using it (1 Shard) briefly flares your sight to 3x; everything the flare touches stays on your
map.

The fog also changes what you meet. Where you can't see right now (dimmed or black), creatures show up more often,
lean tougher and move a little faster. In bright areas they come less often and lean easier.

A **Bonfire** (sold in Armories) clears the fog 15 tiles around the spot where you build it, a tile less each day; one
kit makes ten fires, and a spent kit is rebuilt for 50 Shards. It needs Fog of War on.

### What are the cobblestone roads?

When you raise your Capitol, cobblestone roads join it to every town you hold, upgrading the roads already there. Towns
you restore or capture later join the network. You travel faster on them than on the old sand roads.

## About the game

### Can I play this on Android?

Yes. Every release now includes an Android version (Android 8.0 or newer), installed by sideloading:

1. Download the `forsaken-realms-...-signed-aligned.apk` file from the release on GitHub.
2. Open it and allow installs from your browser or file manager when asked (it's signed by us, not the Play Store).
3. Grant the storage permission it asks for.
4. On first launch, tap Download for the game data (about 220 MB, use Wi-Fi). The app restarts, and after that it
   plays offline.

It installs alongside the official Forge app. Installing a newer APK over the old one keeps your saves. Android gets
less testing than PC, so please report problems on the Discord with your device model and Android version. (There is
no iOS version.)

### Is this a mod for Forge?

No. The Forsaken Realms began as an Adventure-mode world for Forge, but since v1.00 it's a standalone game built on the
Forge rules engine.

- Unzip it into its own folder (not into a Forge install) and run `The Forsaken Realms.exe`. It needs Java 17 or newer
  (64-bit). Windows is the tested platform; macOS and Linux launchers are included but untested.
- It keeps its own saves and settings, so your Forge install is untouched, and the other way around.
- On PC it shares Forge's card-art folder, so card images you already downloaded in Forge aren't downloaded again.

### How do I update the game to the latest version of Forge?

You can't, and you don't need to. Each release is pinned to one tested Forge build, named in its release notes.
Forge's own updater is switched off on purpose, because a different engine underneath can break your world. Engine
updates come to you with new Forsaken Realms releases.

To update the game itself, download the new release zip from GitHub and unzip it (a fresh folder is cleanest). Your
saves live outside the game folder and carry over. On Android, install the new APK over the old one; the app also
tells you when a new version is out.

## Help and bug reports

### How can I help?

See the #how-can-i-help channel on the Discord: https://discord.gg/TTRPKc9HYJ

### Where are my saves and logs? How do I report a bug?

On Windows everything is in **`%APPDATA%\ForsakenRealms`** (paste that into File Explorer's address bar). Saves are in
its `adventure\The Forsaken Realms` folder. The log is **`forge.log`**. Each time you start the game, the previous log
is renamed `forge.<date>-<time>.log` (the last 10 are kept).

To report a bug, post on the Discord: what happened, your difficulty, and the `forge.log` from that session (grab it
before restarting, or pick the matching dated file). Screenshots and your save help too.

- macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/ForsakenRealms`
- Linux: `~/.forsakenrealms`
